What the score means

A complete estimate is easier to compare and enforce.

The tool rewards written details that reduce ambiguity. It cannot measure craftsmanship, honesty, communication, or the condition of work that has not started.

Scope and materials

What is being built or repaired, what is excluded, and which products or allowances are included.

Money and changes

Total price, deposit, milestone payments, and written approval before extra work changes the price.

Risk and responsibility

Insurance documentation, applicable credentials, permits, access, cleanup, and disposal.

Closeout and proof

Warranty language, completion expectations, project photos, references, and final questions.

Use good judgment

Price is only one part of the decision.

A careful comparison checks whether each contractor priced the same work. One estimate may include demolition, disposal, permits, repairs, or premium materials that another estimate leaves out.

  • Confirm the legal business name and contact information.
  • Verify insurance directly from current documentation.
  • Check licensing or registration rules that apply locally.
  • Ask how deposits, progress payments, and changes are documented.
  • Do not rely on this tool as legal, financial, engineering, or safety advice.
